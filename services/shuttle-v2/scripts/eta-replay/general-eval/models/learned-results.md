The direct remaining model wins the narrow aggregate objective but fails the user's initial-total concern. Its initial-total error rises 41.35→45.04 seconds, and initial forecasts more than 120 seconds early rise 4.13→7.39%. For waits lasting at least 300 seconds, its average remaining prediction bias is −132 seconds. A good average across many short waits is insufficient evidence for promotion.

The coherent duration models learn one distribution at arrival and derive every later forecast by conditioning that same distribution on continued waiting. Medium improves Red stop 11's first-total MAE from 164.34 to 91.43 seconds and improves aggregate first-total MAE from 41.35 to 36.33 seconds, but increases Purple's remaining MAE from 48.18 to 53.49 and CRPS from 36.63 to 42.98. Small has slightly better overall CRPS but still loses interval coverage against the existing prior. These are development tradeoffs, not held-out claims.

The single additional normalized trial was requested after discovering the first-total failure. It predicts remaining seconds divided by a causal positive-duration cell median, shrunk toward the pooled positive median with n/(n+20). The scale is frozen at arrival. Training weights are multiplied by the scale, preserving the original seconds pinball objective because s·ρ((y−q)/s)=ρ(y−q). This trial modestly improves initial-total MAE but still raises the initial >120-second underprediction rate to 5.31%; it does not resolve the reason the direct remaining family was rejected. Its source and declaration timestamp precede fitting in `normalized-trial.json`.

The duration representation received a separate semantic correction: repeated positive quantiles retain their point mass, known-standing forecasts condition out zero duration even at elapsed zero, and log-survival arithmetic preserves the exponential overdue tail. Original predictions remain available. The correction leaves these development metrics unchanged to printed precision because their actual learned quantiles contain no material repeated or zero masses. Fixed-point, zero-mass and overdue tests pass; the correction is not a fitted hyperparameter.

Portable inference is a completed feasibility artifact. `learned_export.py` exports tree arrays to JSON; `learned-evaluator.ts` evaluates them in Node and provides `predictLearnedQuery(model,profiles,query)`. Inputs are explicit route/occurrence, observed rest clock and prior known departures. It does not look up the current episode or its eventual target. Model/profile hashes must match, the profile fit cutoff must precede the visit, and prior departures must be known before the visit starts. Profiles and all history-derived arrival features remain frozen during a visit.

The original refitted remaining model occupies 2.84 MB JSON /0.90 MB gzip. Its 92,986 raw quantile predictions across 4,894 real and synthetic edge rows match Python exactly; median Node evaluation was 3.95 ms and p95 7.27 ms on this machine. The coherent development medium model occupies 2.65 MB JSON /0.85 MB gzip. Complete total/remaining runtime predictions match Python within 2.3e−13 seconds on 295 explicit queries. The Node causal feature builder matches Python within 1.2e−16 on 522 queries, including a regression that appending a future departure cannot alter features. The full Node entry point, including feature construction, tree traversal and survival conditioning, matches Python within 4.6e−13 seconds on 522 queries; mismatched models and future fit cutoffs are rejected. The distribution law matches Python within 4.6e−13 seconds on 1,167 cases. Eight semantic/causality Python tests and strict TypeScript checks pass. These measurements demonstrate portable inference; they do not establish complete UI accuracy or production browser latency.
