# Development label integrity finding

The Gold first-total regression cannot be interpreted as a clean comparison of model accuracy. Some stored arrival clocks start many minutes after an independently observed rest near the stop. Moving both experts to those clocks would make their coordinates consistent while retaining a corrupted target.

`physical-rest-audit.py` constructs physical events without reading model predictions or stop-visit labels. For every route and bus, it uses the collector's existing 75 m entry, 150 m exit, 14 s repeated-coordinate rest, and 30 s maximum observation gap definitions. Duplicate bus identities break the track. Initially occupied zones and unfinished exits are censored; rests simultaneously near multiple physical stops are excluded. Only after constructing these events does the audit seek a unique recorded same-stop departure within 30 seconds. It does not infer a repeated route occurrence from proximity.

On September 4, 2,752 events matched uniquely. Median entry-clock error was zero, but 129 stored pins started more than 30 seconds after a confirmed rest and 63 omitted more than two minutes. Those large omissions span eight routes. Of the 63, 59 had exactly the same departure timestamp in the GPS construction and stored label. Their median omitted rest was 354.6 seconds, and their median repeated-coordinate duration was 520.2 seconds. This is predominantly truncation of the beginning of a wait, rather than uncertainty about its end. Among 743 matched events with at least a minute of repeated-coordinate rest, 16 had nevertheless been labelled passed. The audit retains explicit coverage exclusions: 967 nearby-stop ambiguities, 22 left-censored events, and 135 missing or ambiguous departure matches.

Gold episode `6f8a7b0972044431db945e5d` illustrates the mechanism. Raw positions enter the 75 m zone at 16:29:41.229 UTC and begin the first resting plateau at 16:29:51.478. The physical and recorded departure agree exactly at 16:42:59.846. The stored pin is 16:41:59.447, producing a 60.399-second label for a 788.368-second physical rest. Another Gold rest lasting 754.531 seconds has a 49.970-second label. These examples were inspected after the all-route construction, rather than used as exception rules.

The existing browser replay also withholds standing-display records until the old stored pin. Therefore changing only the truth attached to its retained rows would still miss earlier first displays. A fair correction must emit every display first, independently of labels, then associate it with independently reconstructed events. Original metrics and predictions remain preserved; a prediction-blind label rule must be frozen before rescoring. No model change follows automatically from this finding.

The general mathematical contract is an absolute departure time D. If a duration expert is trained on S = D − A, it must be translated by the same observable arrival A when combined with a phase expert predicting D directly. The remaining-time forecast is the law of D − t conditional on D > t and the current observations. A browser continuity clock R may help track a shuffle or approach hold, but replacing A with R in just one expert changes the random variable being mixed. If A is unknown, it requires an explicit arrival uncertainty or fallback; a later outcome cannot supply it retrospectively. Repairing the physical labels and verifying the causal availability of A must precede any proposed clock-aligned revision.

Artifacts: `analytic/physical-rest-development-audit.json` contains all matched rows, rules, exclusions, and source hash. `analytic/gold-clock-audit.json` preserves the separate display-clock analysis. The frozen v2 model, its original confirmation outputs, and the prospective query logs have not been changed by this audit.

The cross-family component study still establishes what each frozen implementation predicted, the absence of future-data leakage in that comparison, and its accuracy against the original labels. It does not yet establish that the analytic model is physically more accurate than the coherent scikit-learn challenger: their development proper scores were effectively tied, and the long-wait tails used to distinguish them are precisely where these label omissions concentrate. Likewise, the direct remaining-time learner's original first-total failure is a result against legacy labels, not a final finding about its accuracy against independently measured waits. The original decision and its reasons remain preserved, but should not be restated as a universal rejection of library models.

Several conclusions survive independently of the labels: remaining time must condition on survival; mixture experts must represent the same clock and event; atoms cannot be smeared away; whole visits rather than repeated polls are the independent evaluation units; and choosing a family before confirmation prevents tuning against its results. The analytic model offers a native runtime and coherent total/remainder contract, while the coherent library learner remains a credible alternative. Any stronger physical-accuracy comparison requires the prediction-blind repaired-label replay or prospective outcomes, with all original forecasts and selection history retained.
