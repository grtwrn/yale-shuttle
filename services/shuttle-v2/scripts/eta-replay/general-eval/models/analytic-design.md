# Conditional time given an observed state

The rider needs the distribution of the time until the bus reaches the boarding stop. There are two distinct uncertainties: where the bus is in its route and what it is doing; and how long the remainder takes given that state. This experiment holds the first fixed. It changes the latter's current-stop duration distribution, leaving route geometry, tracking, future-stop pricing and display policy unchanged.

Let S be total time at the current stop, a the observed arrival time, and r = now − a. A valid remaining-time forecast is derived from the total-duration survival function: P(S − r > t | S > r, context) = survival(r + t | context) / survival(r | context). Repeatedly predicting an unconditional duration, subtracting elapsed time from a median, or holding an elapsed-time-independent mixture weight does not satisfy that identity.

The first expert is the existing empirical duration distribution with pooling by route and stop class. It is a useful default because traffic, boarding and unobserved dispatch decisions remain uncertain. The second expert asks whether this occurrence of this route follows a recurring departure phase. Its period is the median interval between consecutive completed same-bus, same-day visits in training. Its current phase uses all earlier confirmed same-day departures. Prior departures are unwrapped by the learned period, so an unobserved lap does not shift the phase by an entire cycle. Prediction-error quantiles around that phase are learned from earlier data. Shifting those errors by the current arrival's distance from the next slot produces a conditional total-duration distribution.

Neither route IDs, stop IDs, bus identities nor a hand-picked layover duration determine which expert wins. For every occurrence, strictly prequential predictions within the discovery data estimate their relative predictive value. CRPS evaluates both calibration and precision. For distributions F0 and F1, the score of their mixture is

CRPS((1 − w)F0 + wF1, y) = (1 − w) CRPS(F0, y) + w CRPS(F1, y) − w(1 − w) D(F0, F1),

where D = E|X0 − X1| − 0.5 E|X0 − X0'| − 0.5 E|X1 − X1'|. Thus the optimal weight is a bounded one-dimensional quadratic fit, not a list of favorable stops. Eight zero-weight pseudo-observations shrink a cell's fitted weight toward its duration prior. This strength is shared across every route and is fixed before evaluating reserved days. Cells without enough phase history retain the prior.

The mixture is formed at arrival and then conditioned on continued standing. Consequently, its effective phase weight changes according to the two experts' surviving probability mass. Each visit receives equal weight in evaluation; repeated polls during a long stand do not create independent evidence. Zero-duration passes remain part of the unconditional-duration population and are never used as evidence that the bus is currently standing. The production comparator conditions even at elapsed zero: its first displayed total is the median given positive standing duration. Pinned passes belong in its calibration distribution; unpinned passes do not, matching the production calibrator.

The first implementation learned the quadratic mixture weight against unconditional duration at arrival. On September 4 it improved first-total accuracy, but did not improve remaining-time CRPS. A small, broad phase component can be harmless at arrival and dominate the posterior after the duration prior has mostly expired. Learning the weight against the wrong surface therefore matters even when the subsequent conditioning itself is mathematically correct. Unrestricted phase predictions also fail badly at ordinary stops: cadence is much less predictable than their short service time.

The second trial fits the same shared family against each discovery visit's average remaining-time CRPS over elapsed 0, 30, 60, … seconds. It conditions at zero as well. For each candidate arrival weight, the training objective uses the posterior weight w S1(r) / ((1 − w) S0(r) + w S1(r)). The scalar objective is minimized numerically with an eight-pseudo-visit quadratic penalty toward the duration prior; neither its grid resolution nor its prior changes between cells. All discovery weights are frozen before development evaluation. Of 232 fitted occurrences, 224 have enough phase history and 43 receive positive weight. There is no manually selected subset.

The general runtime boundary is `analytic-runtime.ts`. It loads the precomputed fit and indexes completed history once per collector version; training belongs in an offline process or worker, not the collector event loop. Every context requires the exact versioned canonical stop sequence and occurrence. Its wire representation preserves the duration prior's original knots, probabilities, and tail hazard, along with phase error quantiles and mixture weight. It also carries the latest departure and history-availability clocks so the browser can reject references that were unavailable at its own observed arrival. The browser constructs the exact mixture at that arrival clock, including positive atoms, then conditions it on elapsed time. A missing pattern, first visit, zero weight, or unconfirmed history falls back to the existing prior.

`analytic-client-hook.ts` supplies those contexts to the actual browser replay using only server `at_stop_since` / `stationary_since` clocks and conservatively confirmed prior visits. It never matches the current episode to obtain an arrival or departure feature. The selected core body is unchanged: an additive named export exposes its existing expert builders to the adapter. `runtime-manifest.json` records this identity check and the integration hashes. Ten analytic/adapter tests cover causal fitting, occurrence separation, survival, positive atoms, exact JSON wire parity, and context fallback; the first 1,000-position browser replay smoke also passed.
