## Immutable prefix and verification boundary

Input is a fresh immutable copy beginning at journal sequence zero and ending after a declared complete newline-terminated record, with all referenced blobs and an exact snapshot of the atomic recorder manifest. A separate `prefix.json` seals the copied journal byte length/hash, record count, final record hash, manifest hash and recorder identity. Its SHA256 must be supplied by the caller from independently retained provenance; a self-consistent chain alone does not authenticate a rewritten entire capture. Copying must never include a partial last line, overwrite a destination, modify the source, infer the end from a concurrently changing manifest, or claim later records are covered. A bounded prefix-copy helper is permitted for hosted synthetic fixtures and later separately approved immutable copies; this task does not invoke it on the live capture.

The reusable iterator first verifies the seal and complete copied journal hash, then validates every sequence/previous-record link and referenced body while yielding records in original order. It yields only records in the sealed prefix. A missing/reordered/altered line, broken chain, changed manifest, missing/wrong/truncated blob, hash/size mismatch, unexpected recorder/schema/origin, path escape, duplicate journal key or inconsistent recorded metadata raises an integrity error. The caller must consume to EOF and receive completion before treating a whole-prefix result as valid; a later failure invalidates that result, never silently truncates it. Do not use output rows from a failed run.

Validate both stored gzip bytes and recovered raw bytes against their independent hashes/sizes. Bound decompression to the recorder's 8 MiB raw limit; require exactly one completed gzip member with no trailing data. Preserve the original raw body rather than regenerating JSON. Allow exact blob reuse across requests; validate each distinct blob once with bounded cache state. Blob paths must be the content-addressed `blobs/<bodySha256>.gz` form, confined to the copied directory with no symlinks. Unreferenced files cannot become replay input.

Recompute recorder JSON/schema/clock metadata using its finite-number/encoding semantics. Preserve HTTP failures, malformed/nonobject JSON, missing ETA, truncated prefixes, unexpected content encoding, empty fleet, request cancellation and deadline errors separately. Transport completeness is exactly status 200 plus completed read plus no error, not fleet validity or ETA validity. A parseable partial/error body cannot be promoted into a successful response. Duplicate keys in a body may match the recorder's last-key-wins metadata but are flagged as ambiguous and are unusable replay input. Unsupported/missing ETA is passed through to the actual frontend attachment rules, never filled or scored here.

Validate finite monotonic request/receipt clocks, receipt >= request, and serial nonoverlap across requests. UTC fields must parse explicitly as UTC. Wall-clock regression or disagreement with monotonic ordering is retained as a clock-unsafe record/segment, not repaired by sorting or shifting. Such input cannot supply a causal replay time or release attribution. Schedule gaps stay distinct positive skipped-slot records and never become empty fleet responses. Preserve receipt, request, forecast and servedAt clocks separately. `manifest.json` is a mutable-status snapshot: its record count/hash may legitimately lag or lead a selected copied prefix. Report that relationship; never let later manifest timestamps establish prefix coverage. If its count equals the prefix, its final hash and complete/incomplete/gap counters must agree. Storage bytes may include unreferenced orphan blobs; do not pretend referenced-body totals authenticate the entire source directory.

## Causal release knowledge and ambiguity

Producer atomicity clarification found while reading the pinned code: `skippedTicks` increments before appending its gap record. A storage/error stop can therefore leave a terminal manifest with more skipped ticks than the journal. Preserve the excess as an **unjournaled manifest gap count**, without inventing its position or a fleet response; response counters and the manifest's record head must still match its claimed sequence. Clock-unsafe or duplicate-key responses are emitted as `unknown` with `replayAdmissible=false`, rather than pretending they were frontend transport failures. A future runner must retain that uncertainty explicitly.

Release observation groups are the recorder's serial successful HTML plus every resource named by that HTML, with the actual preceding health observation retained. Require the complete nonempty bounded resource set and original hashes. Do not combine failed modules with an old bundle, fill a module from a later retry, or combine resources across HTML observations. Failed/incomplete groups remain unknown. Only fixed public fleet, health, root HTML and same-origin `/assets/*.js` records are accepted; the decoder performs no HTTP requests.

A separately pinned proof maps an exact HTML/resource hash set to source/frontend tree. Proof knowledge has its own timestamp. The initial proven baseline is source `05a988194af3c376e5aa5da16682c29f797db2b2`, frontend tree `39e7e9738975f45dfb5c443cc99961a39e9aa4ef`; hosted proof run35693019507 completed at **2026-09-22T06:01:55Z**. Conservatively use that completion as `proofKnownAt`. A mapped observation becomes usable knowledge only at max(complete bundle observation, proofKnownAt). A later-discovered proof or bundle must never retroactively attribute an earlier fleet receipt. Multiple incompatible eligible proofs remain ambiguous.

For each fleet receipt emit only knowledge available by that receipt: previous health evidence, last complete bundle observation, eligible source proof, failures/build changes since that observation, and an explicit attribution status. A successful changed health build invalidates carry-forward until a fresh complete bundle group; failed health or incomplete resource capture also remains ambiguous. Equal health endpoints do not prove the interval contained no deploy, or that a briefly deployed release was never rolled back.

Therefore a prior matching bundle is a **candidate under an explicit continuity assumption**, not proof of current identity at every later fleet request. The iterator exposes `assumptionRequired=true` and `strictIdentityKnown=false`; it does not silently convert that candidate into the existing adapter's accepted release mapping. A later health record may emit a newly known bracket diagnostic (`equal observed ends`, `changed observed ends`, or failed endpoint), but cannot rewrite the earlier receipt's causal attribution. Any runner that adopts the no-unobserved-intermediate-release assumption must pin that choice separately. A source/bundle proof establishes code identity, not contemporaneous server model training causality.

September 22 boundary clarification, before any prospective execution: root pinned that separate synthetic continuity policy at [`6e2a569`](https://github.com/grtwrn/yale-shuttle/blob/6e2a569/research/public-fleet-timeline/PROSPECTIVE-VERSION-POLICY.md). A later successful same-build health observation can resolve an isolated health failure under that assumption, using the previously complete bundle. Candidate `knownAt` is at least that later health receipt; the intervening unknown interval is never filled. Keep cumulative `healthFailuresSinceBundle` even after this resolution. An observed different build still requires a fresh complete group. Neither a failed resource nor an unpaired resource may carry an old bundle forward.

The event iterator emits a `release-state` for every request and schedule-gap record, including explicit unknown/invalidation states. Each has the original `sequence`, `trigger`, `observedReceiptMs`, `knownAt`, `clockUnsafe`, stable `id`, `shadowsEarlierReleaseMappings=true`, `acceptedRelease=false` and full `releaseEvidence`. Only a `candidate_under_continuity_assumption` state has a top-level candidate source/tree/files; unknown states set those fields to null. File descriptors retain hashes **and** byte counts and are deliberately not the envelope's accepted mapping format. Fleet records link their exact state by `releaseStateId` and `releaseStateSequence`; the state precedes that fleet event. Never filter unknown states out of a version history and then take the latest surviving supported version. An eventual runner must gate the exact initial receipt, as the separate policy requires. Already-bound episodes keep their frontend.

Release-state `knownAt` is the current observation time, while nested candidate `knownAt` identifies when its prerequisite proof/bundle/health knowledge became available. Wall-unsafe release states and diagnostics have null `knownAt`, preserve their observed wall value, and still shadow earlier mappings in journal order. They cannot be filtered away just because their causal timestamp is unknown. UTC-derived milliseconds preserve recorded fractional precision; a future JavaScript runner must explicitly pin its clock conversion instead of silently rounding these evidence fields. The present envelope refuses unknown/nonadmissible receipts, pending that runner's separately reviewed uncertainty policy; it must not convert them into frontend failures.

Timestamp/schema boundary for the later spool: every yielded request, receipt and gap timestamp has already parsed as explicit UTC. `clockUnsafe` means **parseable** wall time with noncausal ordering. Missing or unparseable timestamps are integrity errors that invalidate the whole sealed prefix, not nullable-time records that may pass verification. The decoder's unsafe flag is permanent through the prefix; the spool independently freezes its last-safe coverage frontier at the first unsafe sequence, even if later timestamps appear to recover.

## Hosted synthetic gate and deferred work

Fixtures freeze synthetic append-only prefixes and exercise incomplete final journal lines; mutation/reordering/deletion/rewriting; bad seal/head/count/metadata; shared blobs; raw/gzip hash or length corruption; truncated/multiple-member/oversized gzip; path/symlink rejection; malformed/nonfinite/duplicate JSON; non-200 and parseable partial bodies; serial/monotonic failures versus wall-clock regressions; manifest ahead/behind/equal counters; explicit gaps; failed same-build resource retries; changed builds, rollback and equal brackets; source proof discovered later; unknown and conflicting proofs. A small hosted integration passes verified synthetic fleet receipts to the existing envelope contract without activating any candidate or physical/outcome label.

This task produces a reusable verification/event iterator and conservative release evidence. It does not read real future bodies, fit/score arrivals, clear encounter barriers, infer every intermediate release, or implement/run the scaled 42-scenario replay.
