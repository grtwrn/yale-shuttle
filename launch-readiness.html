Ready to scale the 200-user question

Your flyctl settings need no change. 200 riders polling every 5 s is 40 req/s, and at ~100 ms service time that is only about four requests in flight against a concurrency limit of 200. The machine sits at 0.06 load with 128 MB of 985 MB used. Capacity was never the risk — correctness was.

Two constraints to know rather than fix

You cannot scale horizontally. SQLite on a single volume, plus a collector that polls upstream every 5 s, means a second machine would double-write and corrupt the calibration stats. Growth is vertical only — which is fine well past 200.

Every deploy is a brief outage. One machine, no redundancy. Boot takes ~12 s, inside the 15 s health-check grace period.

How accurate is the arrival time? measured, two ways

Prospect/Canner → the New Haven Green, the real 2.2 km trip. Measured independently by watching the live app tell a rider when the bus would come and recording when it actually did, and by replaying 90 days of history. The two methods agree: the estimate is good, and it is systematically optimistic.

Live: 45 minutes, 4 real arrivals, 35 scored predictions

A browser sat on the live site with the phone's location at the stop, scraping the ETA a rider is actually shown. A separate 5-second poll of raw bus positions recorded the moment a bus came within 45 m of the stop. Nothing was shared between the two.

median |error| 1.26 min  ·  mean |error| 1.44  ·  p90 2.62  ·  worst 3.28  ·  within 2 min: 71%

The estimate sharpens as the bus closes in — exactly the right shape

Error is not uniform: it collapses as the bus approaches, which is what a rider checking their phone at the stop actually depends on. But the bias is negative at every horizon and scales with it — roughly a quarter of the remaining time.

under 2 min out: 0.51  |  2–5 min: 1.19  |  5–10 min: 1.96  |  10+ min: 2.95   (mean |error|, minutes)

Historical: 2,321 real rides over 90 days

The ride leg itself — 13 stops from Prospect/Canner to Temple/Grove. The app predicts 12.15 min; buses actually take a median of 12.83 and a mean of 13.21. And the calibration is earning its keep: the raw sample mean for this time bucket is 11.47 min and the raw median 10.17, so shrinkage pulls the served estimate up, closer to truth than either raw statistic.

error vs median −0.68 min (−5%)  ·  headway at that stop: median 15.5 min, p90 23.6

BiasBoth methods say the same thing: it runs fast

−5% on the ride leg, and −20–25% of the remaining time on the wait. A rider is told the bus is closer than it is. That is the safer direction to be wrong in — you arrive at the stop early rather than watching it pull away — but it is a real bias and worth correcting once the detector fix below has fed cleaner data into the calibration for a week or two.

Shipped and verified

Every item below was proven against production after deploy, not just reasoned about. Three new browser harnesses live in scripts/ so these stay checkable.

The location dot stopped updating while riders walked to their stop

Four of your fourteen reports were this one bug. A battery saver added for report #23 gated high-accuracy GPS on goTrip || boardedRide, which classified walking to the pickup stop — the single most location-sensitive phase there is — as idle browsing. Those riders got a coarse fix up to 60 seconds stale. Worse, it applied from mount: the high-accuracy watch started, then was immediately torn down and replaced with the coarse one, every session.

before watch(high) → clear → watch(low, 60000)  ·  after one high-accuracy watch, no churn

Buses appeared to travel backwards, and ETAs swung by ten minutes

Reports #32, #37, #38 and #41 share one root cause: at_stop_id was trusted over GPS with no ordering check. Many routes pass two stops that nearly coincide but sit far apart in sequence — Broadway/York and Elm/York are 23 m apart yet 2 stops apart; Orange/Pearl (N) and (S) are 35 m apart yet 9 stops apart. Metres of GPS noise relocated a bus a third of a lap. Fixed in three places: the collector now requires a bus to be within 75 m before calling it "at" a stop, and both client anchors now let at_stop_id refine the GPS scan rather than override it.

live buses claiming a stop from 166–558 m: 5 of 16 → 0 (max now 36 m)

Two public endpoints were serving reporter IP addresses

GET /api/reports had no auth and returned the clientIp column — campus IPs alongside each rider's free-text complaint and device details, to anyone who asked. POST /api/reports/{id}/update was worse: no auth, no rate limit, and unlike your other POST routes no body limit, so anyone could wipe the triage log. Both now require a shared secret, compared in constant time, and fail closed when unconfigured.

unauth read 401  ·  unauth write 401  ·  authed read 200  ·  no clientIp in rejection

A phone on the wrong timezone saw "no shuttles running"

The schedule is published Eastern Time, but the checks read getDay() and getHours() — the device's timezone. A visitor still on their home zone had every weekday route judged out of service. Separately, comparing the hardcoded windows against 8 days of observed arrivals showed they run narrower than real service (Pink from 04:00, not 06:00; Grocery from 07:00, not 10:00) — and this filter deletes buses app-wide, so it now fails wide rather than hiding a bus you can see out the window.

identical service state across 5 device timezones (ET, UTC, London, Tokyo, LA)

The trip planner disagreed with your own server — and the server was right

Both map-bot reports reproduced live. For #35 the server returned a fine 53-minute walk while the UI showed a bare "No trip options found"; the client's walk model put it over an hour and suppressed the only option it had. For #40 a filter discarded any trip whose walking legs alone exceeded the direct walk — rejecting the server's recommended 15.9-minute ride by about seven seconds of walking. It now tests true dominance, matching the documented intent that slower options are kept and labelled.

#35 now renders a 68-min walk instead of a dead end

Address search would have died silently at launch

/api/geocode proxied Nominatim with no throttle from two debounced call sites. Their policy is 1 req/s; a few hundred riders typing destinations would have got this machine's IP banned — and a ban just returns empty results forever, with no error. Outbound calls now claim a one-per-second slot, identical lookups collapse into one request, and the User-Agent no longer advertises an app you destroyed in the v2 migration.

My first attempt dropped lookups instead of queueing them, and four test queries came back empty. Caught and reworked before finishing.

burst of 4 distinct queries: all answer, under 1.2 s

Ride tracking had no manual way in

Auto-detect needs a fix good enough to place a rider within 60 m of the board stop, and the manual control had been removed in a card redesign — so when detection missed, the ride page was simply unreachable. Restored as an "I'm on it" action on each expanded shuttle option.

Backend hardening

/api/buses is now memoized as a serialized string — 74% of that 87 KB payload is topology that moves twice a day, and it was rebuilt on all 40 req/s. SSE gained a 32-stream cap, heartbeat and max lifetime (a half-open mobile connection previously leaked forever). departAt became genuinely optional, so its dead ?? now() fallback works again. Shutdown now drains in-flight requests — sized to 4 s because Fly's default kill timeout is 5 s.

SSE cap enforced at exactly 32 in production  ·  hono 4.13.5  ·  drizzle 0.45.2

The planner priced an 8.4 km ride at 97 seconds

Route 9's Orange/Bradley → Building 900 is a genuine 8,204 m hop, the highway run to West Campus. Of its 2,421 recorded samples, 2,411 came in under five minutes — a median of 90 s, or 328 km/h — and calibration served that median faithfully. Across 30 days, 2.5% of all segments imply over 60 km/h, concentrated on exactly these long hops. A duration check can't catch it: 90 s is an ordinary segment time until you know the stops are 8 km apart. Samples implying over 79 km/h are now dropped before any statistic sees them, including the median — which matters, because here the bad samples are the majority.

that trip: 5.1 min → 17.2 min  ·  ride leg 97 s → 823 s  ·  6 segments now fall back to a sane prior

Bus positions were being discarded on the busiest routes

College/Wall (S) and (N) sit 28 m apart but at sequence positions 18 and 28, so a southbound bus flickers between them; the flicker is scored as a 10- or 21-stop jump and thrown away, and it leaves the anchor in the wrong place so the next genuine transition is thrown away too. That is why the final leg into the Green was calibrated from a single observation. A windowed anchor, with constants swept against 59,605 recorded positions, fixes it.

legs with samples 183/232 → 224/232  ·  routes 1, 2, 3 reach 100%  ·  implausible samples 1.9% → 0.9%

The frontend went from 0 tests to 108

TransitMap.tsx is the app and had no coverage at all. Eight pure modules extracted and imported back, behaviour unchanged; every fix from today is now pinned by a test that was mutation-checked — reverted, confirmed failing, restored. The dead Go-trip feature went with it: ~600 unreachable lines, including an /api/accuracy fetch running every 2 minutes for data nothing rendered.

8,309 → 6,763 lines  ·  bundle 505.6 → 499.0 kB  ·  total suite 63 → 235 tests

Calibration no longer freezes the event loop for a second

Grouping in SQL returns 570 rows instead of ~207,000. Order statistics stay in JS because dwells are median + p90, which don't decompose — the exact SQL median was tried and measured slower. Equivalence was proven, not assumed: 358,104 statistic values diffed old against new, 23 differing by at most 1.1e-13 from float summation order.

production 1058 ms → 311 ms every 5 minutes

Deliberately not done

Each of these is a real finding I chose to leave. The reasoning matters more than the list — all of them are safe to pick up next week, and none should be attempted tonight.

CorrectionTwo things I got wrong, and how they were caught

The report #40 fix was inert. I added && totalSec >= directWalkSec believing a filter was discarding a faster ride. It is a theorem that it was not: since totalSec = walkTo + wait + ride + walkFrom, walking further than the direct walk always means slower overall, so the conjunct is unreachable and the original filter could never have dropped a faster option. The diagnosis was wrong, not just the code. The guard is back to its single honest condition with the identity written down, and report #40’s triage note carries the correction.

And my instruction to reconcile the walk model was wrong. I said bring the client in line with the server. Done — and both then agreed on 1.4 m/s over crow-flies, which with this codebase’s own OSRM-measured 1.22 detour is a 6 km/h pace on real pavement. A 2.17 km trip rendered as a 25-minute walk. The old client value was nearly right and the server was wrong. Corrected to 1.1 on the server with the client mirroring it, and walk.test.ts now parses the server’s constant out of its source so they cannot drift apart again.

walk estimates: 1 km now 15.2 min, was 11.9  ·  both planners agree, and are honest

LatentWest Campus is still modelled out of order in the client

Routes 9 and 10 carry duplicate stop ids for the out-and-back. The server side is now fixed — index-based traversal with minimum-forward-distance — but the frontend still dedupes with if (!seen.has(sid)) and then calls indexOf. It currently answers West Campus trips sensibly, so this is latent rather than active; fixing it properly means allowing repeated stops in a sequence, which touches every indexOf call site.

Design“Plan for later” uses right now’s travel times

Calibration is bucketed by day-of-week and hour and recomputed every 5 minutes, which is correct — but segmentTimes carries no time awareness, so a trip planned for 8 PM is priced with midday numbers. For the main route that is a ±12% error. At service boundaries it is much worse: route 2’s 6 AM bucket runs +101% because depot pull-outs are mixed into the samples.

UpstreamBlue Night and Orange Night polylines are wrong

Investigating "the green route is wrong" (#33) cleared Green: 4,000 historical positions sit a median 2 m off the drawn line, p99 47 m. The genuinely bad geometry is Blue Night (60 path points, p90 612 m off) and Orange Night. That is upstream under-sampling — the honest options are to live with it or snap those two lines once and cache. If a "route looks wrong" report recurs, expect one of those two.

WatchTwo remaining sources of ETA movement

When the pinned bus becomes uncatchable, the app re-pins to the next arrival — which on a single-bus route is often the same vehicle a lap later, so the number jumps a full loop. And with the origin box blank, every GPS fix re-runs the planner, which needs a distance hysteresis. Both need judgement about behaviour, not just a patch. Report #34's primary cause (the anchor bug) is fixed; reopen it if drift persists.

Shipped after the audit same day

Work that came out of questions asked once the audit was done — each one turned up something the audit had not.

You can now see how many people use it

There was no way to know. The app logs no requests, has no analytics, and Fly reports request volume rather than visitors; the only person-shaped data was the IP on a submitted report. npm run riders now reports unique browsers for today / 7 d / 30 d / all time, from one row per (day, random browser id) — no IP, no user agent, no coordinates, no time of day.

43 requests from 3 riders wrote exactly 3 rows  ·  ~200 writes/day, not 3.5M

The README described software that isn't running

Flagged as one wrong sentence; every claim was wrong, because it described the archived v1 stack — Python server, MTR schematic, wrong region, wrong database tables. Its deploy section would have walked a contributor into deploying v1 over production, which mattered a great deal more once the repo went public.

Three routes rendered in one colour, from four different tables

Orange Day, Night and East all painted #E65100, and two of them run concurrently. Fixing it found four independent colour tables, two of which had already drifted unnoticed — painting Blue Weekend and Blue Night in Blue Day's blue. One source of truth now; the rest derive from it.

worst simultaneous pair ΔE 0.0 → 12.2, holding under colour-blindness simulation

The Yale tracker link is back, and the bus keeps its identity

v1 could open the operator's own view of a route inline; the rewrite lost it. It's back on each route card — and usefully carries Yale's service alerts, which this app doesn't have. The frame is deliberately not granted geolocation, which v1 did.

Separately: TransLoc reissues bus_id per service block, so the detector was dropping a running bus's anchor ~31 times a day. It now keys on the vehicle name, guarded — and against live production data since deploying, samples implying impossible speeds fell from the historical 2.50% to 0.70%.

calibrated segments 297 → 319 in production — legs that were being discarded

CorrectionMy estimate was wrong, and the obvious fix was worse than the bug

I put the id-reissue cost at ~31 lost segments a day. Replaying 76,134 real positions showed every multi-minute feed gap coincides with a reissue — a bus going off the air at a shift boundary, not a mislabelled one. So naive name-keying inherits the anchor across the layover and bills standing time as travel: +6,733 s and 12 corrupted samples in 6.6 hours. The honest gain from correct keying is ~4 a day.

Launch-day watch list

Where to look first if something feels wrong tomorrow.

  • Usage npm run riders — unique browsers, today / 7 d / 30 d / all time. Counts browsers, not people: phone + laptop is two.
  • Health curl -s https://yale-shuttle.fly.dev/healthz — watch collectorLagMs, and pollSkipped, which is the only outward sign upstream latency has crossed the poll interval.
  • Unproven The bus-id reissue path has never run in production. Reissues happen ~31×/day; none occurred in the observation window. Well tested and guarded, but the first real one is worth watching.
  • Visible Walk times are ~25% longer than yesterday. Correct — the old model implied a 6 km/h pavement pace — but returning riders will notice.
  • Known Wait predictions run optimistic by 20–25% of the remaining time, so the bus arrives later than promised. Safer direction to be wrong; needs cleaner post-fix data before correcting.
  • Triage The queue needs your token, in ~/.yale-shuttle-admin-token. Commands are in CLAUDE.md. Annotate after every fix — that log is the next session's starting point.
  • Deploys One machine, so each deploy is a ~10 s outage. Ship between class changes.
