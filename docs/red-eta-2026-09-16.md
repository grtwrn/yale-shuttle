# Red arrival uncertainty — September 16, 2026

The trip card keeps an **About 6 min** estimate above its full **Likely 4–9 min** prediction window. Tapping opens the interval, shuttle identity, stops to pickup, any observed hold, the following shuttle's estimate, and the estimated gap. The gap is the difference between arrival estimates, not an independently calibrated gap interval. “At your stop” requires observed stop presence.

This is a prediction window targeting 80% coverage, not a confidence interval for a population mean. Holds can make arrival times asymmetric or multimodal. The UI draws the known interval and estimate without inventing a bell curve. Uncertainty can legitimately increase at a hold; the early bound helps riders decide when to reach the stop. [Kay et al., CHI 2016](https://idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2016-WhenIsMyBus-CHI.pdf) supports communicating uncertainty for this decision, without establishing one universal best display.

## Fixes

- The old headline switched between a bare point and a range at its 15-minute width cap. Today's watcher recorded `in 9, 23 min → 2–17 min · 2 buses → in 9, 23 min` within 20 seconds. The new summary retains both the estimate and window; the next arrival is separate in the disclosure.
- Conformal widening previously stepped at 2, 5, 10 and 30 minutes. Adjacent factors now blend over ±30 seconds. The client, nightly scorer and band diagnostic share the function. It removes that discontinuity without suppressing changes caused by new observations.
- Nightly replay omitted `lapB/M/N`, although production uses them. Replay now loads and emits lap fits, includes their 90-day history, excludes departures after the calibration cutoff, and uses new cache filenames. A September 16 midnight patch emits two Red lap cells and three Blue Night cells. Older cached pairs without lap effects are not reused.
- Canary parsing and the rider simulator read the new summary. The continuous runner activates the focused trip card with Enter, so a centre click cannot accidentally open the nested arrival disclosure. The server's Docker image includes the shared ESM helper; an import-closure test checks this.

## Measurements

Baseline: `51c3071bcd13`, latest master fetched at the start. The frozen September 16 archive ends around 10:26 ET: 51,812 raw positions, including 6,957 Red positions. Separately, 1,236 Red watcher polls from 06:59–10:27 ET produce 3,453 bus frames.

Comparisons use a **fixed 10:26 calibration payload**, with served parameters `fit-2026-09-15`. These are paired retrospective checks, not a reconstruction of every historical calibration. Proximity and detector outcomes both derive from upstream GPS, not independent physical observations.

At watcher stop 48, every point prediction is identical between arms. Among 1,549 matched predictions, detector interval coverage stays 96.9%; median width changes from 774.3 to 773.0 seconds. Endpoint changes of at least 60 seconds beyond elapsed countdown decrease from 130 to 125 (1,735 consecutive comparisons); changes of at least 120 seconds decrease from 51 to 50. This modest improvement does not mean the remaining jitter is solved.

GPS replay samples every sixth poll and the next five stops. The same raw pairs are scored through the old and new widening functions, for positive predictions up to 30 minutes and nonnegative outcomes:

| Population / truth | Pairs | Coverage before → after | Median width before → after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Red / detector | 5,420 | 84.0% → 84.3% | 335.6 → 335.6 s |
| Red / 50 m proximity | 5,463 | 86.7% → 86.8% | 330.6 → 330.8 s |
| All routes / detector | 38,062 | 82.6% → 82.8% | 411.8 → 412.8 s |

The paired rider simulation schedules 232 starts at all 29 Red stops. It produces 203 comparable waits: 188 observed arrivals, 12 buses already at the stop, and 3 timeouts. Outcomes and bus selections are identical. First-sight raw interval coverage is only 66.5% in both arms, below the 80% target; the cold-start/planning cohort remains a limitation. Display-drift scores from this run are omitted because the old parser let broad intervals mask visible point jumps. The parser now measures the visible point when present, with a regression test for overlapping windows.

Broadness depends strongly on stop and state. The watcher-stop result does not justify shrinking every Red interval. No new fitted coefficients are published by this change.

## Lap and spacing covariates

A separate causal feature screen evaluates remaining stand 20 seconds after a detected stop at Winchester (11) or Union (121). Features are observed lap age, time since another bus last departed (at least 60 seconds old), and directed GPS distance ahead/behind converted to seconds using a 3,035-second loop. These spacing terms are **distance proxies**, not future observed arrivals or the app's next-shuttle predictions.

There are 137 complete visits on September 11, 14, 15 and 16. Models train only on earlier days; 103 visits are held out on September 14–16, including 14 today. A fixed weak ridge fits each stop separately. All arms use the same complete cases.

| Features | Held-out mean absolute error | 90th percentile absolute error |
|---|---:|---:|
| Stop median | 187.2 s | 382.6 s |
| Lap duration | 119.1 s | 282.9 s |
| Lap + previous departure gap | 120.1 s | 279.9 s |
| Lap + GPS spacing ahead/behind | 121.2 s | 268.6 s |
| All features | 123.6 s | 269.3 s |

Lap duration reduces average error by 36%, consistent with the existing served covariate. Adding spacing does not improve average error here; the smaller tail error merits more days of evaluation. This is a small, selected sample with GPS ambiguity at junctions. It does not establish a dispatch policy or justify new production spacing coefficients.
