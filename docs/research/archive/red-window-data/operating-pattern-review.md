# Operating-pattern covariates: independent statistical review

**We have not explored every useful covariate or operating structure.** The prior experiments establish that own-lap information is useful and that several particular hour, bus, previous-departure and spacing models did not reliably add much. They do not establish that operations lack structure or that richer versions of those features are useless. This is a read-only review; no app, model or production changes.

The official [Yale Red route map](https://your.yale.edu/media/3096/download?inline=) describes three shuttles with offset departures and variable loop durations affected by passengers, weather and traffic. It does not publish exact minute-by-minute departure slots or specify the drivers' holding policy. Offset service is compatible with multiple operating rules; inference from recordings must distinguish them.

## What has and has not been tested

The reconciled [covariate inventory](covariate-inventory.md) remains the authoritative account of the earlier screens. Own lap, another bus's preceding departure, projected physical neighbors, additive hour terms, guarded bus intercepts, weekday, and some historical recency/pace alternatives were considered. `lap-spacing.json` contains 137 observation rows, not 137 different covariates. The current live model already uses elapsed-rest survival, movement/rest state, route progress and lap correction. UI hour matching and recency weighting are separate from fitted live forecasting gains.

The new operating-pattern screen extends that inventory with fixed 15/20/30/60-minute phases, recent own/fleet/opposite-regulator residuals, anchor-to-pin duration and lap×hour/bus×hour interactions. These are sensible small observational hypotheses. They remain components of a stopped-hold model, not a complete model of future rider arrival time. The completed script/results audit appears below.

## Plausible remaining structure, in priority order

1. **One consistent service-episode clock and phase.** The raw-data audit found genuine lengthy waits outside the pin radius and one proven restart-truncated label. Model the attributed approach, pinned wait and departure jointly, or version one broader causal episode origin and remove its waiting time from inbound-leg pricing. Pre-pin elapsed time may be highly predictive because the phases trade off, without showing any new dispatch policy. A component trained on pin→departure cannot safely subtract elapsed time since a much earlier approach rest. This data/target definition issue deserves priority over adding more weak predictors.
2. **Departure slots or service-block rhythm, with exceptions.** Fixed clock harmonics test particular smooth periodic effects. A real “wait until release time” rule would have a sawtooth/threshold shape, possibly with an unknown phase, per-service-block reset, and exceptions for lateness or breaks. Neither an additive hourly sine nor a fixed negative lap slope exhausts that hypothesis. Learn any slot period/offset solely on training dates, compare a small predefined grid and pooled fallback, and evaluate later actual departures. Do not infer a timetable or choose its offset from the test departures. The earlier rejected hard `period−lap` rule also shows that full compensation must not be assumed.
3. **Fleet configuration and interacting buses.** Three scheduled shuttles do not establish that three fresh, active buses are present at every forecast. Plausible features include as-of active fleet count, service entry/exit or relief regime, previous actual departure, and credible ahead/behind arrival gaps at a regulator. Older spacing used nearest path projection and a fixed loop conversion; it did not fully test tracked route occurrence, temporal spacing, changing fleet composition, or nonlinear bunching response. Features must use only prior fixes and departures with freshness/availability guards. Another bus's eventual next arrival or unfinished hold duration would leak future information. Joint prediction must propagate uncertainty in both buses' arrivals.
4. **Recent shared operating regime.** Recent completed travel/hold residuals may detect congestion, demand or a changed dispatch rhythm without claiming to identify the cause. Keep separate own-bus, same-stop fleet and opposite-regulator signals, with count, age and explicit missingness. A previous same-bus residual may encode a driver/service-block effect; a fleet residual may encode traffic, a detector/software change or a common label error. Shrink sparse histories and check benefits across whole days. Earlier failed speed/pace shortcuts do not rule out all such state models.
5. **Stop/pass and duration interactions.** A model fitted only to completed stopped visits says nothing about whether the bus will stop at a future location. Stopping probability may depend on hour, bus/fleet state, route progress or service phase; positive hold duration may depend nonlinearly on lap and elapsed survival. Keep the zero/pass mass separate and evaluate both processes. Within-stop motion/shuffle state and attributed approach state are already tracked but have not all been jointly estimated with time, spacing and survival. Feed deadband/freshness must not be confused with actual physical stillness.
6. **Calendar, traffic, weather and demand context.** Academic term versus recess, weekday class-release patterns, events, road incidents, weather, passenger load and driver/shift assignment are not exhausted. Some are not available causally in the present data; bus number is an imperfect proxy for driver or shift. A few September dates cannot separate these from date-specific fleet composition. Gather/version inputs only for a concrete hypothesis and enough later dates; indiscriminately adding many external variables would increase selection noise.

## Causal and statistical traps

**Shared timestamp identity is not evidence of a driver's rule.** Here `lap = pin − previousDeparture` and `hold = departure − pin`. A pin shifted later by δ automatically increases lap by δ and decreases hold by δ, even if the underlying physical behavior is unchanged. The already documented pin/approach partition makes this important. Negative dependence can still be predictively useful, and its size need not be explained solely by clock error; it just does not by itself identify regulation. Validate later actual departure and end-to-end arrivals from earlier snapshots, alongside duration-component scores.

**Feature availability must precede the forecast.** Completed-history residuals may enter only after the relevant departure was confirmed. A fixed 120-second delay is a conservative sensitivity assumption, not a recovered publication timestamp. Pre-pin approach duration is unavailable before the pin and can be known only after the pin is detected; historical backdating is not real-time availability. For predictions upstream of a future stop, use a causal projected/marginalized feature rather than the eventual realized lap, arrival hour or approach duration.

**Partitioning time is not independent data.** Visits within a bus/day share assignment, weather, traffic and policy. Residual calibration on two days cannot establish conditional 80% coverage across hour, fleet and rest age. Small bus×hour cells should not be treated as independent bus effects; unseen/insufficient buses need a common fallback. A growing catalog of phase/interaction arms increases the chance of a lucky winner. Preserve all results, candidate definitions, penalties and support counts, and freeze one candidate before later confirmation.

**Chronological does not mean untouched.** September 14–17 have repeatedly informed debugging and model decisions. Fixed coefficients trained before September 10 and separate September 10–11 residuals prevent direct fitting on these outcomes, which is valuable. They still make this a retrospective development comparison. Any internal tuning should use earlier date blocks; final test dates must not determine hyperparameters, feature selection or claimed calibration. Within-day test updates using already-completed outcomes can be legitimate online inputs, but should be explicitly distinguished from a frozen daily model.

**Survival and censoring matter.** Complete pin-duration regression does not automatically predict remaining time after 5–10 minutes stopped. Test fixed elapsed snapshots among episodes that survive to them, retaining long/incomplete episodes as censored or unsupported instead of treating a recording cutoff as a departure. Legitimately short remaining waits are not faulty just because they are tails. Proven restart truncations require provenance-aware repair/quarantine; outcome-based removal of short holds would bias both the fit and comparisons.

**Component gains need the actual served route.** Regression on completed holds can improve while making upstream forecasts late or introducing an arrival-only jump. The prior conditional-hold experiment demonstrated this directly. Preserve the candidate's approach-to-rest continuity, stop/pass mass, future covariance and causal clocks; then run the same continuous warm replay, including source checkpoints, departure response, both target occurrences, proper interval scores, directional errors and availability. A small rare-tail tradeoff can be acceptable when typical errors and WIS improve; a systematic center shift should not be called an outlier.

## Practical decision standard

Use the bounded screen to identify one or two coherent hypotheses worth a full replay, not to certify an optimal feature set. Compare every extra family against the same lap baseline on identical supported cases and on the whole eligible cohort with fallbacks. Report per-stop, per-date and sparse/unknown-feature results, not only pooled error. Count physical bus episodes and dates, retain both interval tails, and distinguish improved width/WIS from nominal coverage claims.

The most useful next structural experiment is a consistently defined causal episode plus an explicitly specified release/remaining-time model. A credible clock-phase or recent-regime effect can then be evaluated on top of that definition. The current evidence supports neither “all possible covariates were tried” nor “drivers certainly follow fixed slots.”


## Completed screen audit and numerical findings

I inspected `operating-pattern-screen.py` and independently verified its saved results with [operating-pattern-review.py](operating-pattern-review.py); [the audit JSON](operating-pattern-review.json) includes the input hash, checks and per-date phase/score results. The audit passes: 535 distinct target IDs; correct physical duration arithmetic; completed-history joins precede each query; correct own/fleet/other-stop membership; fit and calibration labels complete before later partitions; identical test cohorts across all 14 arms; ordered nonnegative output quantiles; and independently recomputed MAE/WIS. The script opens SQLite read-only and does not publish anything.

There are **four fit dates** (September 3, 4, 8, 9), **two residual-calibration dates** (10, 11), and **four inspected evaluation dates** (14–17). Winchester has 102/58/99 fit/calibration/test visits, Union 112/61/103. Only 87/97 fit visits and 86/92 test visits respectively have supported lap values; every unsupported case is retained with the pooled fallback and that arm's residual correction. The known restart-truncated visit 65237 is explicitly excluded on concrete evidence, not because its hold is short. The other 37 exclusions are grouped as incomplete, passed, gap-resolved or unsupported pin, so the cohort remains conditional on completing a stopped visit.

The most interesting new result is the fixed **15-minute first-harmonic clock term**, fitted as sine/cosine of the *pin time* along with lap. It adds a roughly 50–54-second harmonic amplitude to the raw fitted center before calibration; it is not a hard departure-slot rule.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Winchester | 99 | 107.4 → 97.9s | 88.1 → 76.4s | 230.9 → 207.2s | 68.4 → 63.7 | 330.1 → 327.5s | 12 / 7 → 11 / 7 |
| Union | 103 | 129.7 → 120.4s | 103.6 → 87.9s | 318.7 → 331.4s | 86.0 → 83.9 | 375.9 → 350.6s | 21 / 5 → 20 / 5 |

The gain is not confined to one good date. MAE improves at Winchester by 2.7/14.1/13.7/7.1 seconds and at Union by 8.9/7.2/6.8/17.1 seconds on September 14/15/16/17 respectively. WIS also improves at both stops on each date. Yet Union p90 error worsens overall and on three dates. Its 25 misses among 103 predictions leave **75.7% coverage**, with 20 early and only 5 late misses. These are not calibrated conditional 80% bands; the early-tail imbalance matters for later rider-facing use.

The 15-minute family is not a universal winner across scores. Union's 30-minute arm has lower WIS 82.9 than the 15-minute arm 83.9, largely with a wider band, while its MAE 128.8 is much worse than 120.4. The other added features are mostly small or mixed. Preserve the complete table rather than choosing whichever of 14 arms wins a favored metric. Bus×hour has worse MAE/WIS at both stops in this fit; that does not identify or exclude driver/shift behavior, and only sufficiently represented bus identities get fitted terms.

**Departure-phase evidence supports a structured hypothesis.** For 15-minute phase, circular resultant length R (0 means diffuse first-harmonic phase; 1 means identical phase) and circular mean phase in minutes after a quarter-hour are:

| Stop | Training R / phase | Evaluation R / phase | Evaluation per-day R range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winchester | .626 / 2.18min | .704 / 1.97min | .584–.829 |
| Union | .722 / .46min | .689 / .59min | .612–.742 |

Concentration is present on every individual fit and evaluation date, rather than created solely by pooling them. Winchester's evaluation mean phase ranges 1.32–2.35 minutes; Union's ranges .36–1.05 minutes. This is descriptive repeated clock alignment. It is **not** a bus frequency/headway estimate, proof that departures always occur there, a known driver instruction, or proof that 15 minutes is the fundamental period. For example, longer cycles with multiple offsets can produce the same 15-minute harmonic. A formal independent-observation circular significance test would overstate evidence here because bus laps and daily schedules are dependent. I did not fit a departure offset using the evaluation data.

### Specific interpretation limits in this script

- The prior-departure lookup is legacy departure time with an assumed 120-second availability delay and same-day/opposite-stop ordering guard. It does not use actual historic receipt times. Historical residuals use `ready <= pin` and therefore no unfinished current/future hold outcome. During evaluation, previously completed evaluation-day holds are allowed to become online covariates; that is causal under the assumption and is not a completely frozen daily feature stream.
- The shared lap baseline used to construct training residuals was fitted on the whole fit block, not with nested chronological out-of-fold predictions. That is an in-sample generated-feature limitation; it does not introduce evaluation labels into coefficients. Before selecting a residual-state architecture, recreate the feature's actual production fitting/update protocol or use internal chronological cross-fitting.
- `prepin` is `max(0, pinned_at − anchored_at)`, not measured motionless time or the live filter's broad rest elapsed. In the audited long-approach examples it is 340, 525 and470 seconds, while the previously measured filter-origin→pin times were 320, 540 and480 seconds. Its log-linear weak result does not reject a coherent multi-phase episode model. Historical pin time can also be backdated relative to when pinning became known.
- All extra feature effects are disabled when own lap is unsupported; those rows receive the pooled fallback. Consequently a weak fleet/hour result does not test whether that feature could help service-entry or missing-lap cases on its own.
- The comparison refits a local additive lap baseline, recenters each model with its separate calibration residual median and adds residual bounds. It is **not** a direct before/after comparison with the production scaled marginal CDF. Calibration data here adjust the point center as well as the interval, which the description should retain.
- All observations are completed stopped pin durations. The 15-minute feature has not been tested for pass mass, residual survival after a long wait, off-marker waiting, the uncertainty in *future* pin time, continuous approach/departure transitions, or later target arrival. A future-stop implementation must evaluate clock phase from its causal simulated arrival distribution, not look up the eventual observed pin time.

**Updated recommendation:** clock-phase structure is a defensible lead for the next bounded component/full-path experiment, because the incremental typical-error and WIS gains repeat across these dates and are accompanied by stable descriptive departure alignment. Freeze this hypothesis, preserve the alternative-arm results, align the duration clock and future-feature contract, and evaluate its actual rider forecasts before any model promotion. This screen answers part of the user's operational suspicion; it does not exhaust the possibilities or establish a published schedule.
