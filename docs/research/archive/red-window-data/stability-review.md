# Position continuity, waiting, and ETA stability

Independent read-only review, 2026-09-17. No application changes or deployment.

**The user's clarification is correct: forward progress of the estimated bus position is a different constraint from a nonincreasing ETA.** The existing running-minimum ETA rule should not be justified by route monotonicity. However, the measured no-ratchet arm also fails the current release criteria; removing that rule by itself is not a sufficient repair.

## What position monotonicity guarantees

For a correctly identified route occurrence and lap, requiring estimated progress `s(t+1) >= s(t)` can prevent a displayed position from moving backward within that occurrence. It does **not** prevent a large forward leap, choosing the wrong branch, a mistaken lap wrap, an incorrect waiting/departure decision, or a revised estimate of future holding time. Continuity additionally needs plausible forward increments and explicit route-occurrence/lap identity. A bus may physically reverse inside a yard; that does not mean it has reversed its sequence of served stops.

On folded routes, several route occurrences share coordinates. Committing to one scalar progress and never permitting re-acquisition can preserve the wrong branch. Monotonic constraints are safer within each plausible branch/visit hypothesis than across the final inferred position regardless of contradictory evidence. A display may hold its marker under ambiguity, but that is a presentation choice, not evidence that the bus stopped or that its arrival distribution narrowed. A purely visual interpolation should not feed invented intermediate positions back into the estimator.

Even at an unchanged position, elapsed waiting is new evidence. If a hold duration is `S` and it has lasted `r`, the remaining distribution is conditional on survival: `P(S-r <= x | S>r) = [F(r+x)-F(r)]/[1-F(r)]`. A heterogeneous hold population need not have a decreasing residual median. Therefore allowing an ETA to increase can be statistically appropriate while estimated route progress remains unchanged or advances.

The saved production forecast at 2026-09-17 14:51:45 UTC predicted 149 seconds to Division/Prospect; the subsequent detector outcome was 527.9 seconds away. The source Winchester visit lasted 500.1 seconds from pin to departure. These are one observed journey and a detector arrival proxy, not a calibration estimate. See `report115-score.json` and `report115-final-outcome.json`.

At `web/src/eta/arrival.ts:817–823`, the retained point is `min(previous ceiling, current mixture point)`. The same negative difference translates every distribution quantile and is clipped at zero. Thus a temporary departure/repositioning mixture can lower subsequent predictions even after evidence returns toward continued holding. Keeping quantiles ordered does not make this translation a valid conditional distribution. A zero lower bound can result from translation; it is not necessarily evidence of immediate physical arrival.

The floor is not the whole defect. Current pricing already conditions residual standing time and recognizes some repositioning (`arrival.ts:192–221,475–501`); the filter already mixes standing/moving and bidirectional shuffle possibilities (`filter.ts:689–741`). Another “add a mixture” proposal would duplicate existing behavior unless it identifies how the new latent state or likelihood differs.

The [no-ratchet screen](ratchet-screen-review.md) held beliefs identical and confirmed `ceilingArmsOnStanding() === false`. Across nine Winchester source visits on one date, removal introduced pickup upward absolute-arrival jumps above 60 seconds on four visits, with a 273.6-second maximum, and worsened first-post-departure pickup MAE from 72.8 to 119.7 seconds. This is a valid paired mechanism warning. It is not a causal accuracy estimate: all nine test visits were included in the fixed empirical stand table, and the replay reset state on nine watcher runs. No immediate production change is supported by that screen.

## Two bounded candidate experiments

### 1. Conditional hold duration, using one consistent clock

Replace the multiplied unconditional hold distribution for a named layover with a nonnegative distribution conditional on the lap observed when the visit began. A small prototype can use a positive duration model with a monotone lap effect and shrunk empirical residuals; fit only training dates and retain the pooled fallback outside support. Explicitly choose pinned-to-departure or rest-to-departure duration and use the identical origin for training, elapsed survival, and live lap. Do not silently mix those clocks.

For an ongoing visit, sample `S-r | S>r, lap` rather than scaling the entire unconditional spread. For a future layover, the lap must be calculated separately on each sampled path from a departure already known at prediction time; never substitute the future realized lap. Test the conditional distribution first, and sample-wise future lap propagation as a separate ablation, so their gains are not conflated.

This targets the poorly conditioned remaining-hold distribution identified in the earlier analysis; it cannot by itself guarantee correct departure classification. Retain the existing movement response in the first experiment. Compare candidate distributions both before presentation and after the current presentation transform. Then assess removing the transform with the final distribution, rather than declaring the earlier no-ratchet failure solved by assumption.

### 2. Separate completion of the layover visit from physical motion

The testable difference from the current standing/moving mixture is an explicit **visit still open versus visit completed** hypothesis that can coexist with physical movement inside the layover. A yard reposition is moving but may retain the same unfinished hold; genuine departure is moving and completes it. Preserve the original visit clock and branch identity separately from the instantaneous motion mode. Preserve both hypotheses through ambiguous fixes, with likelihood updates from the causal sequence rather than a permanent first-fix decision or a mandatory radius/run threshold.

Use the conditional duration hazard from candidate 1 for the open-visit branch. Fit the continuation/reposition and departure observation likelihoods on earlier labeled sequences with conservative shrinkage. A fresh departure-consistent fix must be able to transfer mass immediately; do not require two fixes or a 125-metre exit. Conversely, a return/repeated fix may increase the open-visit posterior without creating a new full hold. Price the current mixture without persisting a minimum from a past mixture. This allows recovery but does not pretend recovery can never revise an ETA upward.

This is a more substantial model experiment, **not a proposed quick production patch**. Existing shuffle/rest logic may already capture most of its value; an explicit state adds complexity only if it improves held-out likelihoods and rider metrics. Departure responsiveness is a release constraint to measure, not a guarantee supplied by the word “probabilistic.” Compare with no-ratchet alone and the current model. Do not restate the previously rejected “restore the standing estimate later” display rule as this candidate.

## Replay and promotion requirements

1. Preserve report 115 and prior shuffle, departure-collapse, approach-rest, folded-route, and first-service-lap recordings as diagnostic regressions. These previously inspected cases are not the final holdout. No cosmetic interval clipping or selective removal of short observed journeys.
2. Replay the actual observation cadence continuously from a causally valid warm checkpoint, or warm up on earlier available observations. Preserve track identity, collector/filter clocks, missing-feed gaps, and model/table refresh times. Ten-second watcher samples are useful diagnostics but cannot establish a five-second first-departure response.
3. Version empirical tables, parameters, lap fits, source code, and caches separately. Each fitted label must be completed **and available** before its cutoff; honor confirmation delays and explicitly mark unknown legacy receipt times. The previously used morning-final payload fails this requirement.
4. Use chronological independent service-date blocks for training, tuning, calibration of the **final candidate's actual output**, and untouched testing. Keep all predictions and both target stops from one source visit together. Freeze feature definitions, penalties, and gates before test; obey existing minimum-date and grouped-validation promotion blocks.
5. Score remaining arrivals at fixed elapsed hold checkpoints and predeclared origin/progress/horizon states. Include first fresh departure fix and +5/+15/+30/+60-second snapshots, raw-posterior trough duration, recovery, false departures, branch errors, and missing/pass-through outcomes. Distinguish true physical movement from detector confirmation latency. Raw GPS can corroborate a stop, but does not prove door opening or boarding.
6. Require point error, interval width and both early/late tails, proper scores, absolute-arrival revisions, and missed connections. Count affected source journeys and service dates, not every correlated poll as a new trial. For position claims, report backward/implausible forward moves and branch-recovery latency separately from ETA monotonicity. Reject a candidate that obtains stability by suppressing necessary departure updates.

**Recommendation:** pursue the conditional hold-distribution experiment first; retain the explicit visit-completion model as the narrowly defined next state experiment. Neither a monotone map position nor the existing no-ratchet diagnostic earns a model rollout. The evidence rejects several shortcuts, while leaving room for a better causal, conditionally scored model.
