# Covariates actually explored: reconciled inventory

Read-only review, 2026-09-17. **We have tested several useful feature families, not all possible covariates or combinations.** Evidence ranges from descriptive associations to chronological component tests and older full-estimator replays. These are different levels of evidence and should not be presented as interchangeable validation.

## Correction to the previous summary

**Physical spacing to buses ahead and behind was constructed and screened.** The later [covariate-followup.md](covariate-followup.md) sentence “No ready, validated next-shuttle covariate was constructed here” refers to that particular follow-up script. It must not imply that the earlier physical-spacing experiment never happened. The older September 11 evidence audit predates the September 16 spacing experiment, so its “not tested” statement is historical.

In [lap-spacing.json](../red-eta-data/lap-spacing.json), `features: 137` means **137 complete visit observations**, not 137 different covariates. The actual numeric predictors are lap age, prior-other-bus departure age, projected forward spacing, and projected backward spacing; models are fit separately for two stops.

## Physical-spacing screen: exact scope and limitations

The saved data contain 137 visits on September 11, 14, 15, and 16: 65 Winchester, 72 Union, five bus names. At each visit, the target is remaining stand **20 seconds after the recorded pinned time**, conditional on the visit still continuing. Forward folds train on earlier dates only: 34→50, 84→39, and 123→14 visits, giving 103 test observations across three later dates. From the saved labels, all training departures precede the first observation of their next fold; this check does not reconstruct exact publication times.

| Model | Test MAE seconds | p90 absolute error seconds |
|---|---:|---:|
| Stop median | 187.2 | 382.6 |
| Lap | 119.1 | 282.9 |
| Lap + previous departure | 120.1 | 279.9 |
| Lap + projected ahead/behind spacing | 121.2 | 268.6 |
| Lap + previous departure + spacing | 123.6 | 269.3 |

Lap+spacing MAE also worsens within each stop: Winchester 134.37→135.59 seconds (49 tests), Union 105.16→108.07 (54). Thus a pooled stop mix does not explain away the lack of average-error improvement.

Limitations verified in the script:

- It selects complete cases. Exclusions logged include 295 missing fresh watcher frames, 15 missing acceptable laps, three missing recent prior departures, and two missing projected neighbors. Other early skips are not counted. It also selects on recorded `stand_sec` between 15 and 1,800 seconds, so it does not represent every service situation.
- A frame may precede the evaluation time by up to 20 seconds and have reported global feed age up to 20 seconds. Buses share that frame, but this is not proof of synchronized per-bus physical GPS fixes.
- “Directed” describes modulo distance in route-path order. The implementation uses nearest-polyline projection, without heading, historical branch tracking, or stop-occurrence disambiguation. Junctions, crossings, and repeated path segments can assign the wrong neighbor. With only two buses, ahead and behind may refer to the same other bus.
- The prior-departure feature assumes 60 seconds makes an event available. It does not use historical receipt/confirmation timestamps. Own lap comes from the saved live payload, which is a stronger availability record.
- These models use fixed weak ridge regression. They score point MAE and p90 absolute error; there are no separate interval calibration dates, proper distribution scores, rider connection outcomes, or held-out continuous warm-model replay here. Three dates and dependent bus visits support a hypothesis screen, not calibrated accuracy claims.

## What the inventory does not establish

The ten original shortest-journey inspections came from already time-matched history fixtures; they were not an all-day sample. Their morning source times cannot establish that short trips occur only in those hours. The parent is separately performing the all-day hour-stratified check.

No reviewed experiment exhausts weather, external traffic, signal phase, passenger load/boarding, driver/shift identity, dispatch decisions, school-calendar/service regimes, or every interaction among available predictors. Those inputs need causal availability and enough dates before testing; adding them indiscriminately would encourage overfitting.

The defensible conclusion is: **lap is the best-supported tested hold covariate; 15-minute clock phase is a promising new component-model lead; bus/hour and geometric neighbor spacing have shown mixed or weak incremental gains.** That is a statement about these particular models, labels, dates, and metrics. Next work should improve causal outcome/feature provenance and departure/remaining-wait modeling, then use fixed date-separated fit/tune/calibration/test stages, bus-journey grouping, both interval tails, proper scores, and rider failures. No current result supports claiming that every useful feature has been exhausted or promoting a new coefficient from the inventory alone.

September 17 follow-up: [release-investigation.md](release-investigation.md) extends the clock finding to per-bus/day hourly recurrence, a causal prior-two-departure phase feature, and a departure-hazard model with elapsed survival. The hazard gives materially larger historical remaining-wait gains than the additive pin-time phase regression. Actual recorded-wire testing also exposes a non-deployable direct-switch regression at pin entry and departure; neither improved component scores nor an inferred hourly role alone establishes a served ETA improvement. Separate per-age versus one-CDF calibration experiments are retained with their limitations. These additions still do not exhaust active fleet, interacting neighboring arrival gaps, driver/shift identity or demand context.
